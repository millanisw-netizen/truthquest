📋 DOCUMENTED INCIDENTS

When Fake News Turned Deadly

These are real events. Disinformation isn't a game — but understanding it can save lives.

HIGH IMPACT
2016
🍕

Pizzagate — The Shooting That Never Should Have Happened

🇺🇸 Washington D.C., USA

The lie: A viral conspiracy claimed a Washington D.C. pizza restaurant was secretly running a child trafficking ring connected to senior Democratic politicians. The claim spread via social media and fringe websites, citing fabricated "coded messages" in hacked emails.

What happened: On December 4, 2016, a man drove from North Carolina to the restaurant armed with an assault rifle and two handguns. He fired multiple shots inside while "self-investigating." No victims were harmed physically, but the restaurant staff lived in terror for months. The owner received thousands of death threats.

💡 MIL lesson: Fabricated "evidence" in the form of screenshots and creative interpretation of normal text spread unchecked across platforms. The claim was debunked by every major news organisation — but the believers never saw the corrections.
Sources: BBC AP News
GENOCIDE LINK
2017
📱

Myanmar — Facebook and the Rohingya Genocide

🇲🇲 Myanmar (Burma)

The lie: For years, coordinated networks of Facebook accounts — many linked to the Myanmar military — spread fabricated stories portraying the Rohingya Muslim minority as violent criminals, terrorists, and threats to Buddhist society. The content included fake images, manufactured atrocity stories, and dehumanising language.

What happened: A UN fact-finding mission concluded that Facebook played a "determining role" in spreading hate speech that contributed to violence. In August 2017, military operations forced over 700,000 Rohingya to flee to Bangladesh. Mass killings, sexual violence, and village burnings were documented. The UN described it as bearing the hallmarks of genocide.

💡 MIL lesson: Social media platforms with no local language moderation allowed coordinated disinformation to operate unchecked for years. For many Rohingya, Facebook was the internet — there was no competing information ecosystem to provide context.
Sources: Amnesty International UN News
LIVES LOST
2018
📲

India WhatsApp Lynchings — Forwarded Messages, Real Deaths

🇮🇳 Multiple states, India

The lie: A WhatsApp voice message and video claimed that strangers were kidnapping children in local villages. The message — entirely fabricated — spread rapidly through rural WhatsApp groups, often forwarded dozens of times before reaching recipients who had no way to verify it.

What happened: At least 31 people were killed in mob lynchings across India between 2017 and 2019, directly linked to false child abduction rumours spread via WhatsApp. In one documented case, five Muslim men from Hyderabad were beaten to death by a mob of over 200 people after being falsely identified as kidnappers. They were on a charitable visit distributing food.

💡 MIL lesson: End-to-end encryption means disinformation in private messaging apps is nearly impossible to monitor or correct at scale. The "forwarded" label on messages is a weak signal — it cannot convey how many times false information has been amplified.
Sources: Washington Post Wikipedia
INFRASTRUCTURE
2020
📡

COVID-19 + 5G Conspiracy — Towers Burned Across Europe

🇬🇧🇳🇱🇧🇪🇮🇪 UK, Netherlands, Belgium, Ireland

The lie: A conspiracy theory falsely claimed 5G mobile phone towers were causing or spreading COVID-19. The claim — which has no scientific basis — spread through YouTube videos, Facebook posts, and celebrity endorsements, reaching millions within weeks of the pandemic's start.

What happened: More than 100 arson attacks on 5G infrastructure were recorded across the UK and Europe in early 2020. Telecom engineers were harassed and physically attacked. In some cases, engineers working on completely unrelated equipment were targeted. Hospitals relying on those networks faced disruption during the height of the pandemic.

💡 MIL lesson: In a crisis, people seek explanations — and disinformation fills that vacuum faster than facts. The BBC published debunking articles, but the conspiracy's emotional appeal ("they're making us sick") outpaced corrections in algorithmic feeds.
Sources: PBS NewsHour BBC
VIOLENCE SPARKED
2019
💬

Sri Lanka — Social Media Shutdown After Attacks Fuelled Riots

🇱🇰 Sri Lanka

The lie: Following the Easter Sunday bombings that killed 269 people, false rumours and manipulated images spread rapidly on Facebook, WhatsApp, and Viber, falsely blaming Muslim communities and inciting retaliation. Fabricated claims about Muslims stockpiling weapons spread within hours.

What happened: Riots broke out in multiple cities. Mosques and Muslim-owned businesses were attacked and burned. The Sri Lankan government took the drastic step of blocking all social media platforms nationally for several days — a decision criticised for impacting press freedom but seen as necessary to prevent further bloodshed. At least one person was killed in the subsequent communal violence.

💡 MIL lesson: In the immediate aftermath of real tragedies, the disinformation environment is at its most dangerous. Grief and fear lower critical thinking thresholds — making verification more important, not less, precisely when it is hardest.
Sources: Reuters BBC
POLICY IMPACT
2019
🌳

Amazon Fires — Old Photos Weaponised for Political Narratives

🇧🇷 Brazil

The lie: During the 2019 Amazon wildfire crisis, viral social media posts shared dramatic images of burning rainforest — many of which were decades-old stock photos or images from fires in other countries entirely. Some were AI-manipulated. These images were shared millions of times alongside emotionally charged, sometimes misleading claims about causes and responsibility.

What happened: The Brazilian government and international leaders publicly clashed based partly on the distorted information environment. Celebrities and world leaders shared false images in good faith, amplifying confusion. Meanwhile, the actual scale of fires — which was genuinely alarming — was simultaneously overstated and distorted by the false imagery, undermining trust in legitimate environmental reporting.

💡 MIL lesson: Even when sharing content you care deeply about, reverse image search takes 10 seconds. Sharing false images for a true cause still spreads disinformation — and hands ammunition to those who want to discredit the entire story.
Sources: BBC AP Fact Check

What is Media &
Information Literacy?

Media and Information Literacy (MIL) is the set of skills that allows people to access, analyze, evaluate, create, and act using all forms of communication. In a world of viral misinformation, algorithmic feeds, and AI-generated content, MIL is not optional — it's survival.

UNESCO champions MIL as a fundamental right. Informed, critical citizens are the strongest defense against the manipulation of public opinion. TruthQuest puts these skills in your hands — through play.

✅ Recognizing manipulation tactics
✅ Understanding digital footprints
✅ Ethical information sharing
✅ Detecting AI-generated content
